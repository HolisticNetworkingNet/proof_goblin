# The Philosophy of Proof Goblin

## Why Proof Goblin Exists

Proof Goblin began with a simple problem.

For much of a professional career, there was nobody else in the room.

Not because organizations failed to value review, but because many technical professionals work alone within their discipline. A software engineer may be the only software engineer. A technical writer may be the only technical writer. A researcher may be the only person capable of evaluating a specialized system. Independent developers, consultants, educators, and open source maintainers often publish important work without the benefit of experienced peer review.

The result is not a lack of expertise.

It is a lack of perspective.

Proof Goblin exists to reduce that isolation.

Its purpose is to provide thoughtful review from multiple professional perspectives before work is published, shared, or released. It is intended to augment human judgment, not replace it.

The goal is not automation.

The goal is better thinking.

---

## Review as Conversation

The best reviews are conversations.

Experienced reviewers rarely notice the same things for the same reasons. Each brings years of practice, habits, and instincts to the work. One notices ambiguity. Another notices security implications. Another notices assumptions that will confuse new users. Each contributes something valuable that the others might not have seen.

Proof Goblin seeks to recreate that experience.

Rather than producing a single authoritative opinion, it approaches review as a conversation among specialists examining the same artifact from different perspectives.

Agreement between perspectives can increase confidence.

Differences between perspectives can expose hidden assumptions.

Both outcomes are valuable.

---

## Perspectives, Not Personas

Proof Goblin does not ask artificial intelligence to impersonate people.

Instead, it applies **Proof Lenses**.

A Proof Lens represents a disciplined way of reading an artifact. Each lens focuses attention on particular kinds of questions while allowing other concerns to remain in the background.

Just as a security engineer naturally notices trust boundaries, or a technical writer notices ambiguity, each lens has learned what deserves attention.

The artifact does not change.

Only the perspective changes.

---

## Every Review Begins with a Question

Reviews exist to answer questions.

A security review asks whether unnecessary risk has been introduced.

An FAQ discovery review asks what readers will still wonder after reading.

A release readiness review asks whether a document appears ready for publication.

Proof Goblin expresses these objectives as **Review Missions**.

A mission gives purpose to a review. Every participating lens works toward the same objective while contributing its own perspective.

---

## Observations Before Conclusions

Proof Goblin values careful observation.

Each review produces observations—individual things that a particular lens considered worthy of attention while pursuing a particular mission.

Observations are not judgments about the author.

They are invitations to think more carefully.

The responsibility for deciding what to change always remains with the human author.

---

## A Tool for Better Thinking

Proof Goblin is not intended to replace thoughtful collaboration.

Whenever experienced colleagues are available, their insight remains invaluable.

Instead, Proof Goblin exists for the moments when those colleagues are unavailable, when deadlines are short, when expertise is isolated, or when an additional perspective would simply be helpful.

Its purpose is not to remove people from the review process.

Its purpose is to make careful review more accessible.

If Proof Goblin succeeds, authors will publish with greater confidence—not because an artificial intelligence approved their work, but because they had the opportunity to consider perspectives that otherwise might never have been present.
